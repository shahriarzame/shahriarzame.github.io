Parcel-Sorting Example: MC Crossing AC

Scenario: A small parcel-sorting station has a fixed equipment cost of €4 per shift and a basic variable handling cost of €1 per parcel. Up to 4 parcels, the station works efficiently. Beyond 4 parcels, congestion and overtime increase the extra cost of each additional parcel.

Equations

FC = 4
VC(q) = q + 0.5 [max(0, q − 4)]2
TC(q) = FC + VC(q)
AC(q) = TC(q) / q
Continuous MC(q) = dTC/dq = 1 (q ≤ 4); q − 3 (q > 4)
Discrete MC(q) = TC(q) − TC(q − 1) (whole parcels)

Why are the two MC values different at q = 5? The whole fifth parcel adds €9.50 − €8.00 = €1.50 to total cost. But the instantaneous slope at q = 5 is €2.00. The red curve uses the instantaneous derivative, so it intersects AC at the continuous minimum.

The two approaches describe different quantities: a finite increase of one parcel versus a slope at a point.
